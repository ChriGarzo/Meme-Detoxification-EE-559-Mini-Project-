    \begin{block}{Problem definition}
    Harmful memes combine images and overlaid text to spread offensive content
    across both online platforms and physical spaces. Existing moderation
    relies mainly on \textbf{censorship} after exposure: removing, blurring, or
    suppressing the content.

    \smallgap
    This project instead proposes \textbf{text detoxification}: rewriting
    harmful meme text in real time while preserving the core meaning and visual
    coherence of the original meme. The goal is proactive moderation without
    erasure, enabling safer message delivery rather than simple deletion.

    \smallgap
    The research hypothesis is that a large multimodal teacher can explain why
    a meme is harmful and generate safer pseudo-rewrites, then transfer this
    context-aware rewriting behavior to a compact BART-large student for
    efficient deployment.
    \end{block}

    \begin{block}{Related works}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Hateful meme detection.} The dominant literature frames
        harmful memes as a classification task. The Hateful Memes challenge
        showed that multimodal fusion models outperform unimodal baselines, and
        later VisualBERT- and CLIP-based systems further improved image--text
        reasoning \cite{kiela2020hateful,li2019visualbert,radford2021learning}.
        These methods detect or remove content, but do not preserve the
        original communicative intent through rewriting.
        \item \textbf{Text detoxification and style transfer.} ParaDetox treats
        toxicity as a style attribute and trains sequence-to-sequence models
        such as BART/T5 to neutralize toxic sentences \cite{logacheva2022paradetox}.
        However, plain-text detoxification ignores the image context that often
        gives meme text its harmful meaning.
        \item \textbf{Our gap.} We use LLaVA-Next's multimodal understanding as
        a teacher to produce target groups, visual evidence, implicit meanings,
        and pseudo-detoxified rewrites \cite{liu2024llava}. These labels are
        distilled into a lightweight BART-large student, enabling
        context-aware rewriting at much lower inference cost.
    \end{itemize}
    \end{block}

    \begin{block}{Method}
    \begin{center}
        \includestandalone[mode=tex,width=0.86\linewidth]{../architecture_diagram}
    \end{center}

    \tightgap
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Stage 0:} EasyOCR extracts meme text; CLIP filters
        non-meme-like images, using stricter negative prompts for MMHS150K.
        \item \textbf{Stage 1:} LLaVA-Next runs in two sharded passes: first
        structured hate explanations, then pseudo-detoxified rewrites.
        \item \textbf{Stage 2:} noisy pseudo-labels are filtered by parse
        validity, Stage 1 quality checks, positive toxicity reduction, and text
        quality before LoRA fine-tuning BART-large.
        \item \textbf{Stage 3:} a proxy MLP maps CLIP image/text features to a
        short BART encoder-memory sequence, exploring a VLM-free deployment
        path.
    \end{itemize}

    \smallgap
    \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{>{\bfseries}p{0.31\linewidth} X}
        Student & \texttt{facebook/bart-large}, 406M parameters.\\
        LoRA setup & Rank 32, alpha 64, dropout 0.05; 17M trainable parameters
        (\(\sim\)4.3\%).\\
        Conditions & \texttt{full}, \texttt{target\_only},
        \texttt{visual\_only}, and \texttt{none}.\\
    \end{tabularx}
    \end{block}

    \begin{block}{Datasets}
    \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{>{\bfseries}p{0.18\linewidth} X}
        HarMeme & \(\sim\)3,500 memes related to COVID-19 and US politics,
        manually labelled as harmful/non-harmful with associated textual claims
        \cite{pramanick2021harmeme}.\\
        MAMI & 10,000 misogynist memes from Twitter \cite{fersini2022mami}.\\
        MMHS150K & \(\sim\)150,000 heterogeneous and noisy Twitter
        image--text posts \cite{gomez2020multimodal}.\\
    \end{tabularx}

    \smallgap
    All datasets are filtered to retain meme-like images only. The final Stage 2
    supervision contains 3,578 clean pseudo-rewrite pairs from 7,918 loaded
    LLaVA rows, split into 3,220 train and 358 validation examples. Combining
    curated and noisy sources exposes the model to diverse toxicity types and
    improves generalization pressure.
    \end{block}

    \begin{block}{Validation}
    \textbf{Quality on 358 validation memes.} Text STA is the mean non-toxic
    probability from a RoBERTa toxicity classifier; $\Delta$ toxicity is the
    improvement over the original meme text; SIM is BERTScore F1; CLIP measures
    image--text alignment.

    \smallgap
    \begin{center}
    \small
    \setlength{\tabcolsep}{5pt}
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.18}
    \begin{tabular}{lcccc}
        \toprule
        System & Text STA & $\Delta$ toxicity & SIM & CLIP\\
        \midrule
        LLaVA-Next Teacher & \textbf{0.9517} & \textbf{0.2804} & 0.3938 & 0.6253\\
        DetoxLLM baseline & 0.8894 & 0.2182 & 0.4202 & 0.6251\\
        BART-large Full & 0.9261 & 0.2548 & 0.2991 & 0.6236\\
        Proxy + BART & 0.8084 & 0.1372 & \textbf{0.5527} & \textbf{0.6312}\\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}

    \smallgap
    \textbf{Reading the trade-off.} BART-large Full gives the strongest student
    toxicity reduction and improves over DetoxLLM \cite{hallinan2023detoxllm}
    on Text STA and $\Delta$ toxicity. DetoxLLM and Proxy + BART preserve more surface
    similarity, but the proxy currently sacrifices detoxification strength. This
    matches the project goal: distil a useful portion of LLaVA's multimodal
    rewriting behavior into a much smaller student.

    \begin{block}{Limitations}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Dataset noise and heterogeneity remain substantial, especially in
        MMHS150K.
        \item The student depends on LLaVA pseudo-labels; filtering improves
        precision but reduces the training set size.
        \item BART has limited direct visual grounding at inference time; image
        context is distilled through text fields rather than consumed as pixels.
        \item Evaluation is only partially multimodal. VisualBERT scores were
        difficult to interpret as absolute detoxification metrics in this run.
        \item The proxy model still shows an unbalanced toxicity--similarity
        trade-off.
    \end{itemize}
    \end{block}

    \begin{block}{Conclusion}
    This project shows that multimodal supervision from LLaVA-Next can be
    distilled into a lightweight BART-large student that performs
    meme-specific text detoxification: it reduces toxicity while preserving
    semantic alignment and image--text coherence.

    \smallgap
    The result supports a shift from reactive censorship to constructive
    rewriting. Future work should improve dataset quality, strengthen direct
    multimodal grounding, and integrate OCR plus inpainting modules for live
    rewriting of harmful text in video streams and real-world physical
    environments.
    \end{block}
